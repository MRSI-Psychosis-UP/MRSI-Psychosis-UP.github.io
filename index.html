Research

Translational research into the mechanisms of psychosis.

The primary mission of the Psychosis Unit is to better understand the neurobiological mechanisms underlying psychosis, in order to improve the care of adolescents and young adults suffering from schizophrenia-spectrum disorders or other psychotic disorders.

Since its creation in 2022, our Unit conducts "translational" research. This means that our work draws both on clinical data from patients participating in our studies (for example, blood markers or symptom severity) and on laboratory experiments (for example, experimental disease models using cell cultures). This approach has been applied for over a decade at the Centre for Psychiatric Neuroscience; it was introduced by Kim Do (former head of the Unit for Research in Schizophrenia), in collaboration with the clinical services of the Department of Psychiatry, and has since developed progressively with the support of the NCCR-Synapsy.

We are also strongly committed to training a new generation of young clinician-researchers, some of whom follow an MD-PhD program. Clinician-researchers are essential for bridging the gap between clinical practice and fundamental research, and for maintaining a dialogue between healthcare professionals and patients on one hand, and neuroscientists on the other.

The Unit is therefore composed of a multidisciplinary team that includes both scientists (biologists, a physicist, a biochemist) and clinicians (physicians, psychologists, an art therapist).

Centre for Psychiatric Neuroscience

The Psychosis Unit is part of the Department of Psychiatry and the Centre for Psychiatric Neuroscience at CHUV, the University Hospital of Lausanne. Our translational approach was introduced at the Centre over a decade ago by Kim Do, former head of the Unit for Research in Schizophrenia, in collaboration with the clinical services of the Department of Psychiatry, and has since grown with the support of the NCCR-Synapsy.

CHUV — University Hospital of Lausanne

CHUV is a leading university hospital and research centre in Lausanne, Switzerland, combining clinical care, teaching and research across medicine and neuroscience.
